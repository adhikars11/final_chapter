\opening{Dear Editor,}

I submit ``Resolving Coordination Frictions in Green Labor Transitions: Minimizing Unemployment, Costs, and Welfare Distortions'' for consideration in the European Economic Review Special Issue on ``The Macroeconomic and Financial Aspects of Climate Change.''

This paper was presented at the EER Conference on ``The Macroeconomic and Financial Aspects of Climate Change'' (Banco de México). I have revised the manuscript best to my ability based on feedback from the discussant and conference participants.

The paper demonstrates that coordinated worker-firm policy support dominates one-sided alternatives in green transitions, achieving employment targets with lower unemployment, lower fiscal costs, and higher welfare. It derives dynamic policy rules showing that optimal instrument choice is state-contingent, rotating as binding constraints shift along the transition path. The welfare threshold for net gains is modest, environmental benefits exceeding 0.87\% of consumption, making the results directly relevant for policy implementation.

This manuscript is original work, not under review elsewhere. I have no conflicts of interest to declare. I appreciate your consideration of this work.
